# Four-week research visibility cycle

Use this cycle for one priority paper at a time. The objective is legitimate discovery and reuse—not citation solicitation. Every post should link to the canonical paper, state limitations and identify resources accurately.

| Week | Theme | Deliverable | Evidence to include |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Technical explainer | Diagram, carousel or short video | Research question, method, reported result and DOI or publisher URL |
| 2 | Limits and future work | Concise limitations post | Dataset and setting boundaries, uncertainty and specific research gaps |
| 3 | Data and reusable resources | Resource card | Only public datasets, code, BibTeX and open-access links; distinguish general resources from study releases |
| 4 | Application or co-author spotlight | Use-case story or collaborator perspective | Author roles, intended application and a clear warning against unsupported operational claims |

## Distribution checklist

- LinkedIn: plain-language hook, visual and canonical article link
- ResearchGate: technical summary and accompanying material where appropriate
- Academia.edu: short summary and publisher/DOI link
- GitHub: permanent page, BibTeX and verified resources
- ORCID and institutional profile: canonical publication metadata and hub link where the platform supports it
- Co-authors: invite factual review and voluntary resharing; do not request reciprocal citations

## Monthly review

Record the date, Scholar/Scopus/Web of Science totals, individual priority-paper counts, link clicks where available and any genuine reuse enquiries. Separate platform engagement from academic citations.
